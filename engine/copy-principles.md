# Copy principles: make her people feel seen

Read this before writing any piece, and again when you check it. It sits on top of `brand/voice.md`. The voice file decides how the words sound. This file decides what the words are trying to do.

Every piece has one job: the right reader stops, thinks "she is talking about me," keeps reading, and wants to follow, reply, or take the next step. Content made only to fill a calendar fails that job.

These ideas come from proven direct-response writing (Dan Kennedy, Gary Halbert, Eugene Schwartz, Robert Collier) and from today's conversational copy for coaches and creators. They are tools, not a script. Her voice always wins over a technique.

## The rules that never bend

These come first, because direct copy can slide into hype.
- Everything still comes from the source material and her profile. Never invent a story, a result, a number, a testimonial, or something a real person said.
- A mirror line is the speaker talking TO the reader ("You say you will start Monday."). It is never a claim about a real event or a real person unless the source says it.
- No fake urgency, fake scarcity, or fear. Only use a deadline or limit she gave you.
- Agitate honestly. Name what the problem costs her reader in plain words. Never shame her, never scare her, never promise a result.
- Keep the one next step from `brand/offer.md`.

## 1. Enter the conversation already in her head

Her reader already has a running conversation in her mind. Start inside it, in her words. Do not start with your topic.

- Find the mirror line first: one specific moment, thought, or feeling her reader has. Examples of the shape: "You told yourself you would start after the holidays." "You are the one everybody leans on, and you are tired." Pull these from the source (what the speaker names as the struggle), from `brand/profile.md` (who it is for, the problem), and from any "In her people's words" notes.
- Be specific. "You feel overwhelmed" could be anyone. A real scene, a real habit, a real sentence she has said to herself is her.
- If you cannot find a real mirror line in the material, build it from the teaching and ask the person for two or three things their audience says out loud. Put those in `brand/profile.md` under "In her people's words."

## 2. The first line earns the second

Most people decide in one line. Use one of these hook shapes, and vary them across the pack:
- **Call-out:** names her reader. "If you are the woman everybody depends on..."
- **Belief challenge:** says the thing she half believes is wrong. "You do not need a stage."
- **Named feeling:** says what she feels before she can. "You are tired and you still said yes."
- **Specific scene:** one concrete moment from the source.
- **Question only she would answer yes to.**
- **Plain promise the piece really keeps.**

A good hook is specific, speaks to one person, and makes the next line feel necessary. Cut warm-up ("Today I want to talk about...").

## 3. Pick a shape that moves her

- **Problem, honest cost, way through:** name it, say what it costs her, then give the teaching as the way forward.
- **Story, lesson, step:** only when the source has the story. Then one thing she can do today.
- **Myth and truth:** the belief, then what is actually true.
- **Before, after, bridge:** where she is, where the teaching could take her (never as a guarantee), and the one step across.

Between slides, lines, and paragraphs, leave a small open loop so she keeps going. End on a turn or an action, not a recap.

## 4. Write to one woman

- One reader, one idea, one next step per piece.
- Say "you" at least twice as often as "I."
- Plain words, short lines, the way she would say it at the kitchen table. Specific beats clever.
- Say it once. Cut anything that repeats.
- Benefit before feature: what changes for her, in her life, before how the teaching works.

## 5. Match the stage

Her people are at different points. Match the piece.
- **Reach:** she may not know she has the problem. Lead with the feeling or the belief. Ask for something small (save, share, comment).
- **Warm:** she knows the problem and wants a way through. Lead with the teaching and what it makes possible.
- **Ready:** she is close. Be clear and kind about the next step. Remove doubt with what is true, not pressure.

## 6. Make the next step easy

State it once, plainly, in her words from `brand/offer.md`. Say what she gets, then what to do. In emails, a short P.S. that repeats the one step often gets read when the body does not.

## 7. The mirror test (run it on every piece)

Before a piece is done, check:
1. **Seen:** does a specific line make her think "that is me" within the first three lines?
2. **Specific:** is there a concrete detail, not a general claim?
3. **One:** one reader, one idea, one next step?
4. **True:** is every claim, story, and number from the source?
5. **Her:** would she say this out loud? (Read it against `brand/voice.md`.)

If any answer is no, rewrite the opening from scratch. Do not just swap words.

## Quick guide by format

- **Carousel cover:** call-out or belief challenge in 14 words or fewer. The second slide names her situation.
- **Reel and short video:** line one is the mirror line or the challenge, spoken. No introduction.
- **Thread and notes:** post one stands alone and makes her tap.
- **Instagram and Facebook:** the first two lines show before "more," so make them the mirror.
- **LinkedIn:** first line under 90 characters, one clear takeaway for someone scrolling at work.
- **Email:** the subject line is specific and curious, never clickbait. Open mid-thought, like a note to one woman, and end with one step and a P.S.
- **Pinterest and YouTube:** lead with the exact words she would search, then give her a reason to click.
- **Blog and Substack:** open with her situation, teach one idea completely, then the one step.
